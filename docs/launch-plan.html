CoachUp Kids
Product & Go-to-Market Plan · 90 Days

From prototype to a loved, growing product in one focused quarter.

A phase-by-phase plan to take CoachUp Kids — the playful discovery experience that reveals a child's physical, emotional and social readiness for sport — from its high-fidelity prototype to a public launch and an early retention engine.

Audience · busy, single & sport parents + kids 4–12 Platform · Web/PWA first, iOS & Android wrap Model · Freemium → Subscription + coach marketplace Runway · 13 weeks to public launch

Strategic snapshot

Three decisions shape everything below. Lock these before sprint one.

The positioning wedge

Every other kids-sport app sells a sport or talent. CoachUp Kids sells readiness and confidence before the sport is even chosen — physical, emotional, social and mental. That is the ownable space, and it speaks directly to a parent's real anxiety: "Will my child enjoy this, fit in, and not feel behind?" Hold this line in every headline: not a talent test, not a ranking, not a sport-selector.

Recommended monetization

Freemium is the right front door for anxious, price-sensitive parents: let the value prove itself first. Convert on the ongoing journey, not the one-time report.

AdviceDo not gate the DNA report — it is your growth loop. A parent who shares a beautiful report is your cheapest acquisition channel. Charge for the relationship (seasons, courage, coach notes), never the assessment.

Platform & timeline reality-check

The prototype is already a responsive React/Vite web app. Ship it as an installable PWA first (fastest path, no store review), then wrap with Capacitor for the App Store and Play Store in parallel with launch. A true 1-month build-and-launch is not realistic for a paid, multi-child product — but 13 weeks to a public launch is, because the hardest 60% (the whole experience) already exists as a validated prototype.

1Pre-launch & preparation

Weeks 1–4. Prove the wedge, cut the scope hard, design the flow, and start building an audience before a line of production code ships.

Market research & validation

  • Map 8–10 competitors across three buckets: talent/skills apps, general kids-activity apps, and parenting/child-development apps. Note what each charges and what they don't cover (emotional/social readiness).
  • Run a parent survey (n≈100) via school groups, parenting Facebook communities and Reddit. Ask about anxieties, not features: "What worries you about your child starting sport?"
  • Do 6–8 live parent interviews (30 min). Show the prototype; watch where they lean in and where they hesitate on price.
  • Write a one-page value-proposition canvas: parent pains → CoachUp relievers → the one sentence they'd repeat to a friend.
  • Validate willingness-to-pay with a simple Van Westendorp price question in the survey.
AdviceRecruit survey respondents from the exact niches you named — single parents, sport parents. Segment results by type; a sport parent and a cautious first-timer buy for opposite reasons, and your messaging will need both.

AdviceYour prototype is feature-rich already — the risk is over-shipping, not under. Cut multi-child and the coach marketplace from launch. A focused free→paid loop with one child profile is faster to build, easier to measure, and enough to prove retention.

Design & wireframing

  • Turn prototype learnings into 2–3 tightened user flows: first-run (Discovery→report), the paywall moment, and week-1 of a season.
  • Commission real illustrations for guides and the brand mark to replace emoji placeholders (biggest perceived-quality lift).
  • Run 5 usability tests on the paywall placement specifically — where it sits decides your conversion.
  • Finalize the design system in tokens (colors, type, spacing) so build is assembly, not invention.

Pre-marketing strategy

  • Ship a waitlist landing page in week 1 (the current hero works) with email capture and the "sportiness to light" promise.
  • Lock brand identity: logo, palette, voice guide, social templates.
  • Publish teaser content — short "did you know movement confidence matters more than talent at age 6?" clips for Instagram/TikTok.
  • Line up 10–15 parent micro-influencers and 3–5 schools/clubs for beta and launch amplification.
  • Target: 500+ waitlist emails before public launch.

Beta testing strategy

  • Closed beta (W8): 20–30 waitlist families via TestFlight / PWA link. Tight feedback loop.
  • Open beta (W9): widen to 100–150; watch the funnel, not just opinions.
  • Instrument a one-tap in-app feedback button and triage in Linear/GitHub with severity labels.
  • Define launch-blocker criteria: crash-free > 99%, Discovery completion > 70%, paywall view→trial > baseline.
  • Run a safeguarding review of Courage Curve content with a child-development specialist before public launch.
AdviceRecruit beta families who will actually run the home quests with their kids, not just click through. Retention signal from real play is the only beta data that predicts post-launch churn.

3Launch & go-to-market

Weeks 9–11. Convert the audience you've been building into a concentrated launch moment, then keep the channels warm.

App Store Optimization (ASO)

  • Keywords: target "kids sports readiness", "child confidence", "movement skills kids", "kids activity", not competitive "kids sports" head terms.
  • Title: CoachUp Kids: Sport Readiness. Subtitle: Confidence, movement & fun.
  • Screenshots: lead with the DNA report and the parent benefit caption, not UI chrome. First two frames win the install.
  • Preview video (15–20s): a child laughing through a home quest → the report reveal → a calm parent. Emotion over feature tour.
  • Seed early ratings from beta families the day of launch.

Launch campaign

  • Product Hunt launch (Tue–Thu): rally waitlist + team for first-3-hours momentum; lead with the "not a talent test" angle.
  • PR outreach to parenting and edtech press + local news ("local founder helps kids fall in love with movement").
  • Social blitz across the teaser channels; publish the founder story and the "why we don't rank kids" manifesto.
  • Activate micro-influencers and schools/clubs with a unique code + free Club month to share.
  • Send the waitlist launch email with an early-supporter discount.
AdviceLaunch one concentrated week, not a slow trickle. Concentrated attention drives store ranking, which drives organic installs — the compounding you can't buy. Have every asset ready the Friday before.

4Post-launch growth & retention

Weeks 10–13 and beyond. The launch spike fades in days; the retention engine is what makes this a business.

Onboarding & retention

  • Activation sequence: get every new parent to a completed Discovery Day + report in the first session — that's the "aha".
  • Push notifications: weekly quest reminders in the guide's voice; celebrate streak-free wins, never guilt.
  • Email drip: day 0 welcome, day 2 "how to run a quest", day 7 season nudge, day 14 upgrade story.
  • Churn reduction: win-back for lapsed families; annual Discovery Day "re-run" as a birthday ritual.
  • Referral loop: the shareable report is the invite; add "start your child's journey" to it.
AdviceFor a parent product, the real retention lever is the child asking to play again. Optimize the between-session hook (quest reminders, badges, the guide "remembering" them) harder than any adult-facing feature.

Feedback & iteration — the V1.1 roadmap

  • Weeks 11–12: triage launch bugs daily; ship a stability patch within the first week.
  • Week 13: first retention review — read the D7/D30 cohort and the paywall funnel; decide what V1.1 fixes.
  • Prioritize the V1.1 backlog by impact × effort: likely multi-child profiles, real illustrations, and the coach-marketplace pilot.
  • Stand up a public feature board so parents feel heard and you crowdsource the roadmap.
  • Plan the coach/club pilot — the marketplace is the path from subscription business to platform.

How to read this plan. Weeks are relative to your kickoff, not calendar dates. The four phases overlap on purpose — pre-marketing and analytics never stop, and beta runs while you're still polishing. The single biggest accelerator you already hold: a validated, high-fidelity prototype, which is why a public launch inside 13 weeks is realistic where it usually isn't.

First five moves, in order: ① lock the free-vs-paid line ② ship the waitlist page ③ cut MVP scope with the table above ④ stand up Supabase + Stripe ⑤ recruit 30 beta families. Everything else follows.
